**Role:** Junior Year Project (May 2022 – May 2023)  
**Supervisor:** Dr. Md. Mashiur Rahaman, Professor, Dept. of Naval Architecture and Marine Engineering, BUET

---

**The problem:** Bangladesh's inland waterway transport network relies heavily on diesel-powered vessels, contributing significantly to CO₂ emissions and fuel costs along high-density passenger routes.

**My contribution:** I worked on this in my junior year as part of a two member undergraduate project team. I worked on the conceptual design of a 100-passenger inland vessel with fully electric propulsion system, instead of the diesel setups (still standard across Bangladesh's waterways). We worked through the usual constraints: route length, passenger and crew loads, battery capacity, charging needs etc. As a result, we landed on a hull roughly 23.8 m in length between perpendiculars, 4.5 m in beam, displacing about 64.7 tonnes displacement, with 260 kW of brake power at a 15-knot service speed. The vessel's principal particulars were derived through iterative sizing using established ship-design methods. Then we benchmarked that design directly against an equivalent diesel propelled vessel on propulsion efficiency, structural design, and lifecycle cost.

**Outcome:** The electric setup came out roughly twice as efficient as the diesel baseline (about 85% versus under 45%) and worked out to a diesel equivalent saving of around $0.28 per liter. The catch is upfront cost: charging infrastructure alone accounts for about $1.18 million of the roughly $1.2 million total system cost. This cost is really the barrier to adoption in Bangladesh right now, not the technology itself. The work was published as a co-authored paper in the *Journal of Maritime Research* (2023).
